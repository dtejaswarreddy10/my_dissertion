Recap — The Problem

Production AI silicon needs three efficiency techniques together — precision flexibility, structured sparsity, and per-PE power gating — but no open, characterized RTL combines all three.

3+ precision lanes

No single fixed-precision array is optimal across a whole model.

40–80% activations ≈ zero

2:4 structured sparsity gives a compact, hardware-friendly way to skip them.

60–70% power in the MAC datapath

Operand isolation + clock gating must live in the RTL, not be bolted on.

My contribution: one unified array combining all three, plus a quantified study of how precision mode, sparsity rate, and gating effectiveness interact.

Verification Approach

Every module is checked against a Python golden reference model, at two levels.

PE-level (cycle-accurate)

Every intermediate cycle's output compared against the golden model, with exhaustive input coverage where the space is small enough to enumerate completely.

Array-level (GEMM)

Full matrices streamed through the design; final settled result compared against a golden GEMM (or pruned-GEMM) reference — this is where dataflow, skew, and boundary conditions are actually exercised.

FPGA Results — Interpretation

  • Area isn't apples-to-apples here: baseline maps its MAC to a hardened DSP48; the precision-adaptive PE is deliberately kept in fabric. The ASIC flow (next) is the real source of the area-overhead number.
  • Power trend is directionally right: "Full design" measures lower dynamic power than the otherwise-identical "sparse, gating off" — consistent with operand isolation working — but this is still under a uniform-toggle placeholder, not yet the activity-annotated (SAIF) result.
  • INT4×2 throughput measured at 1.53×, against a 1.8–2.0× target — stated honestly, not smoothed over.
  • Getting here required real debugging: added a pipeline stage to pe_mixed and a feeder output register during timing closure — both confirmed correct via full exhaustive and array-level re-verification afterward.

Design Insight — The Bottleneck Isn't the Multiplier

The critical path on sky130 runs register-to-register through the 32-bit accumulator, not the multiply.

acc_q[1] → xnor2 → o21ai → 14× maj3_1 (full-adder carry chain) → nand4b → ... → acc_q[31]

maj3_1 is the standard full-adder carry cell — 14 of them chained back to back is a textbook ripple-carry adder. Yosys/ABC mapped the 32-bit accumulate this way by default, so its delay scales roughly linearly with word width.

Implication: if more speed is needed later, a faster adder structure for the accumulator — not the multiplier — is where the gain would come from.
